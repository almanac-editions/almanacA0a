**Remark [§4, closing paragraph, printed p. 14 — unnumbered, in running text].** Quoted verbatim from the printed page:

> Let us also briefly mention that in our setup there is another natural choice of generator $z := (q-1)x+1$ for $\mathbb{Q}(q)[x]$. Evaluating $x := [n]_q$ is the same as evaluating $z := q^n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. And $x$ is obtainable from $z$ by the linear transformation $x = (q-1)^{-1}(z-1)$. Thus the ring of all polynomials $P \in \mathbb{Q}(q)[x]$ with $P(q^n) \in \mathbb{Z}[q]$ ($P(q^n) \in \mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]$) for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$) is evidently isomorphic to $\mathcal{R}^+_q$ ($\mathcal{R}_q$). For $q$ specialized to a natural number this "$z$-variable" version of $\mathcal{R}^+_q$ is discussed in [Gra90]. Going one step further, if we formally adjoin square roots $K^2 = z$ and $v^2 = q$, then the ring $\mathcal{R}_q[K,v]$ is equivalent to the Cartan part of Luztig's integral form of the quantum group $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ [Lus89]. Much of the theory developed here can be done just as easily in the $z$ or $K$ variable formulations of these rings. We chose the $x$ variable convention since it highlights the combinatorics and is the most transparent for specializing $q$ to 1.

("Luztig" is the source's own spelling on the printed page; the bibliography entry [Lus89] reads correctly, "G. Lusztig".)

## What this does and does not give us

**Gives, and it is exact.** A node-preserving affine change of coordinate carrying the paper's additive grid to the multiplicative one, together with the resulting isomorphism of the two integer-valued rings. In program variables, with $q_{\mathrm{paper}} = Q = q^2$ and $z_{\mathrm{paper}} = \check{Y}$:
$$\check{Y} \;=\; 1 + (Q-1)\,x, \qquad x \;=\; (Q-1)^{-1}(\check{Y}-1), \qquad x = [n]_Q \iff \check{Y} = Q^n .$$

**Gives, but only as an assertion.** That after adjoining the square roots — which in program variables means passing from $(Q, \check{Y})$ to $(q, K_+)$ — the bilateral ring becomes the Cartan part of Lusztig's integral form for $U_v(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. No proof, no definition of "equivalent", rank one only.

**Does not give.** Any statement in the $z$ or $K$ variables written out in full; any higher-rank statement; any comparison with the program's own weight-Newton lattice under a fixed normalisation. That last comparison remains the open edge recorded in `own-weight-newton-basis-equals-lusztig-toral-lattice` and in `own-sl2-rees-newton-centre-theorem`: this card removes the excuse that the source was unread, and does not by itself close the reconciliation.
